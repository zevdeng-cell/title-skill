# Blog and Search Formulas

Use these for blogs, articles, tutorials, explainers, comparisons, and search pages. Keep one clear promise per title.

| ID | Formula | Best use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `B01` | `[Topic]: [specific angle, format, or proof]` | searchable topic plus clear benefit |
| `B02` | `How to [do X] [qualifier]` | practical tutorial or process |
| `B03` | `[N] [tips/tools/mistakes] to [outcome]` | a real list whose count matches the body |
| `B04` | `What Is [term]? [why it matters or next action]` | definition or explainer |
| `B05` | `[Topic]: The [guide/checklist/template/playbook]` | substantial resource that delivers the named format |
| `B06` | `[search question]?` | query already phrased as a question |
| `B07` | `[A] vs. [B]: [difference or decision]` | genuine comparison with a decision criterion |
| `B08` | `[formula] ([year])` | annually maintained content where freshness matters |

Useful secondary levers include audience, method, step count, proof, honest parenthetical scope, second person, or a freshness marker. Use one authority word at most; stacked words such as “ultimate complete definitive” read as spam.

For English, 45-70 characters or roughly 7-12 words is a useful starting range, not a hard limit. Put the searchable head term early. In Chinese, prefer natural specificity and the publication's house style instead of mechanically translating English structures.

Reject keyword stuffing, vague superlatives, fake precision, misleading years, mismatched counts, thin “ultimate guides,” and copied publisher suffixes.
