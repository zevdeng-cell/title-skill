# Evidence and Performance Review

## Evidence Levels

1. Current official editor fields and content rules may be treated as hard constraints.
2. Randomized experiments or public research transfer only within compatible language, platform, and context; cross-context use is directional.
3. The user's account history supports account-specific associations, not causation or platform-wide rules.
4. Third-party cases and community feedback suggest angles, not universal effects.
5. Writing experience and language instinct generate candidates, not algorithm claims.

Product names, prices, rankings, policies, and year markers are time-sensitive. Verify them when factual currency matters.

## Review Workflow

1. Separate views, plays, click-through rate, completion, likes, saves, shares, and follows.
2. Preserve displayed precision. `10K+` is a lower bound, not an exact value.
3. Build a comparable series using the same account, format, content type, metric, and observation window.
4. When enough data exists, use the median of the latest 3-5 comparable items as an approximate account baseline. Otherwise state that the baseline is unknown.
5. Separate possible explanations into title factors, topic and timing, account and distribution, and unknowns.
6. Say “associated with,” “possible explanation,” or “below this account's recent baseline.” Do not claim causation without an appropriate experiment.
7. Review high and low performers together to reduce survivorship bias.

If impressions differ substantially, prefer click-through rate at a comparable exposure stage. If impressions or observation time are missing, state the limitation.

Inspect the formula or angle, key-term placement, specificity, visible action, intensity, body support, format fit, and opening payoff. Do not assign a numerical title score.
